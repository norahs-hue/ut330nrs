Research methods lab: the coffee shop interviews

October 7, 2026 · Research methods lab

This lab was practice for interviewing. Our case was an independent coffee shop that closes in the late afternoon. Customers sometimes say they want somewhere to study, meet, or hang out in the evening, but the owner is not sure evening traffic would cover the extra labor and operating costs, especially if people stay a long time and buy very little.

Our research question was: what currently determines the coffee shop's hours, and what conditions would make an evening operation viable?

We did not talk to real people. We used the interview simulator prompt from the lab and had ChatGPT play one role each, starting a new chat for every participant.

Each of us interviewed a different person from the shop.

What I asked the manager

Megan runs the day-to-day at the shop: staff, scheduling, and closing. I asked her five things:

  1. How does a typical closing go, and how many customers are still in the shop when she closes?
  2. How much would the business and staffing have to change to stay open later?
  3. How hard would it be to recruit reliable front-end employees for the extra hours?
  4. What currently decides the shop's hours, and what would have to change to change them permanently?
  5. How often does she leave after closing to buy food or drinks somewhere else, and where does she go?

What the manager told me

Closing
They start winding down about 30 minutes before close. One person does most of the cleaning while the other watches customers and finishes orders. Usually three to six customers are still there, and on some nights she has to remind people with laptops that the shop is closing.
Staying later
She called it more of a staffing change than an operational one. They would need at least two people for safety, and shifts are scheduled tightly around closing now, so they would have to extend them or add an evening shift. That means paying for more labor overall, not just two more hours of being open.
Hiring
Applicants come in fairly regularly, especially students, but their availability is all over the place. She thinks they would need a few people to cover the late hours, and it takes time before she trusts someone to close alone.
What sets the hours today
Mostly when they know enough customers will come to make the labor worthwhile. They look at sales by hour, and the owner also has a preference about not keeping staff late when few people are around.
Leaving to eat
Once or twice a week when she closes. She grabs something quick nearby, like a sandwich place a few blocks away or a convenience store.

I don't think we'd change the hours based on one busy week. It would probably take a few months of seeing that the demand is actually there.

Megan, the manager (played by ChatGPT), in my interview

That was her answer to what would have to change for the hours to change permanently. She also wanted enough sales to cover wages, utilities, supplies, and the extra closing time, and a way to staff those hours reliably.

What the rest of the team heard

Labor
The owner said staying open would mean a whole separate evening shift, because the person who opens at 6 AM cannot stay until 8 or 9 PM. His full-timers prefer mornings. Students and people with second jobs are open to evenings but cancel and swap around exams. The barista said two people usually close, and sometimes only one.
Who is in the shop
The cashier said the shop feels fullest around 4 to 6 PM, with people on laptops or meeting friends, and that the last hour brings about 10 to 15 sales. The owner said evening customers tend to buy one $5 drink and stay for hours. The customer said they spend $7 to $9 a visit, stay two to three hours, and would come more often and buy a second or third drink if the shop stayed open until 10.
What might make evenings pay
The owner said events like open mic nights, book clubs, and board game nights are the only time staying late has consistently paid off, but they take a lot of effort and work more like a once a week or twice a month boost. He also said higher-ticket items like wine, beer, or small plates would help. The cashier liked hot chocolate because it is easy to make, said donuts are trickier to stock, and said they would need to check zoning, the business license, and occupancy rules.

Something that did not line up

Putting the notes side by side, the participants did not agree on when the shop closes. The case says late afternoon, the owner talked as if it closes around 4, and the cashier, the barista, and the customer all said 8 PM. Each interview was its own chat, so the participants never compared notes.

What we still do not know. Nobody gave real sales by hour, even though the manager said that is what the shop looks at. The cashier said they would have to check local rules before staying open much later, and the manager wants a few months of evening demand before changing the hours. Those are the gaps I would start with.
